§ III — Process

How a doll begins.

Eleven months from the first sketch to the final stitch. Every step happens in one room; nothing leaves the atelier unsigned.

Atelier — the artist's workbench
Plate VII. Atelier · Shenzhen · 06:42
  1. I.

    The sketch

    A doll begins as a face on paper — sometimes a single feature, sometimes a feeling. Months may pass before the sketch is allowed to leave the page.

  2. II.

    Sculpting

    Heads are sculpted from a single block of clay. Proportions are checked against a small mirror — never a screen — and adjusted in silence.

  3. III.

    Casting & firing

    Porcelain casts are fired three times. Two firings to set the body, one final firing to fix the painting. Roughly one in five survives all three.

  4. IV.

    Face-up

    The painting of the face — the slowest, quietest part of the work. Twenty to thirty thin layers, dried between each, in soft daylight only.

  5. V.

    Dressing & finishing

    Each doll is dressed in garments cut for her alone, in linen, silk, or wool. She is then named, signed beneath the foot, and photographed once.

§ IV — On Aurora

Aurora began in a single room in 2026, with one face that wouldn't stop being drawn.

What we make are not toys, and not exactly sculpture. They are quiet companions — small bodies asked to hold whatever the room around them is holding. Joy, mostly. Sometimes other things.

We move slowly on purpose. A studio of three. Twelve to twenty pieces in a good year.

§ VI — Questions

Asked often.

A few of the most common — written plainly. If something here is missing, the atelier replies to letters within a week.

How are pieces numbered and signed?

Originals are 1/1 and signed beneath the foot, with the doll's name and the year. Limited Editions carry an engraved disc with the edition number — for example 03/24 — fixed to the lower spine. Studio Line pieces bear the atelier mark only.

Can a doll be commissioned?

We accept a small number of commissions each year, only for the Originals tier. The studio writes back to discuss the work in person — by post or by video — before any commitment is made.

What is the wait, once an Original is ordered?

Between nine and fourteen months, depending on the season and the complexity of the work. Limited Editions ship within four weeks of release; Studio Line within ten days.

What materials are used?

Bodies are porcelain or French BJD resin. Eyes are German hand-blown glass. Hair is mostly Angora mohair from a small ranch in Texas. Garments are linen, silk, or English wool.

How are pieces shipped and insured?

Each piece travels in a fitted cedar case, packed by hand. Shipping is fully insured to the declared value and tracked door to door. Customs documentation is prepared by the studio.

Can a doll be repaired?

Yes — and we would prefer that you wrote to us before attempting any repair yourself. Aurora maintains a lifetime restoration service for its own pieces, at the cost of materials only.
